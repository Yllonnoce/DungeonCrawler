Status: written 2026-11-12 for the story rewire. The authority above this page is the design, `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-12-gullet-story-arc.md`; if this page and the design disagree, the design wins and the owner is told.
After the owner reads act 1, their corrections go into these sheets first, then into the lines.

Every writing task on the rewire (floors, taunts, merchant, banter, barks) writes to these sheets. A writer who has never seen the game should be able to write a scene for any speaker from their sheet alone. Two writers working from the same sheet should produce the same person.

Old lines quoted below are from `docs/story/before.md`. Only their voices and habits carry over; the old plot (a town that sends four people down every few decades, a pilgrimage, a contract that is a trap, names carved in advance, Crane's betrayal, a choir, a mine that is the god's body) is gone.

## Rules for every speaker

1. **Say the want plainly the first time.** Every character's first line on their first floor states what they want from the party in words they would say aloud. The want sentence on each sheet is that line or close to it.
2. **No riddles.** Nobody says "you will see" or hints at a secret they will not name. A character who does not know something says so; a character who will not say something says so.
3. **One image per speech at most.** A speech is one line or one dialogue node.
4. **220 characters at most** for anything spoken.
5. **People change when the party learns something.** Each sheet says how its speaker talks after each clue that touches them. The four clues: the wardens' log (floor 4), the dismissal order (floor 9), the moulds (floor 13), the Verger's letter (floor 16).
6. **No NPC, taunt or narration ever names a hero.** Co-op players name their own heroes. Never write Brand, Sable, Wren or Ilex in a line someone else speaks. Address the party as "you", "mercenaries" or "hired swords" (the Herald says "hirelings"). Do not count them ("you four"); a co-op party can be smaller. Placeholders the game fills are fine: `{party}`, `{name}`, `{floor}`, `{prisoner}`, `{elite}`. The heroes still use each other's names in their own banter, as they do today.
7. **Write in the speaker's own sex.** Sable, Tamsin, Ione, Grisel and Maud are women; everyone else is a man. Other characters refer to them with the right pronouns.
8. **Tone: dark, with humour.** The humour comes from people, never from the trapped townspeople's suffering. Nobody jokes at a prisoner's expense except the Herald, and he is the villain.
9. **Delivery.** Each line may carry one delivery: whisper, weary, calm, warm, cheerful, sly, pleading, angry, mocking, shout, booming, frantic. Each sheet gives the speaker's default.
10. **Shared voices.** Three pairs share a recorded voice. Keep them apart by how they talk: Brand and Old Ulf (clipped soldier against chatty old craftsman), Wren and Crane (one or two words against long clauses), Oskar and the Verger (a few heavy words against long, precise sentences). Never put two of a pair in the same exchange.

## What every writer needs to know

### The story in order

1. **Three hundred years ago and since.** Gorm, a good god, sleeps under Dunmarrow. His dreams hold the Sleepless, an evil god, out of this world. The water that runs down through the old mines is what feeds him. The town's drain-wardens have cleared the mine drains every season for three hundred years.
2. **Before this spring.** Absalom Quill, Verger of Gorm's church in Dunmarrow, prayed to Gorm all his life and was never answered. The Herald of the Sleepless answered him. The way down to the mines runs through the church's crypts at the lychgate, and the Verger opened it to the Herald.
3. **This spring.** The Verger ordered "no one below". The wardens' log stops there. With the wardens unable to go down, the council stopped paying them, and Alderman Crane signed the order. The reason on the paper was the Verger's order: why pay wardens who cannot work? The truth, which Crane owns only on floor 17, is that the council was glad of the excuse because it saved money. Some wardens went down anyway, unpaid, to keep the water moving. Grisel Thorn's crew went first. The Herald's minions took them.
4. **Since.** The Herald, Morg Ashtusk, made the mines his base. He raised the church's dead, laid traps and filled the tunnels with minions so nobody could reach the drains. He choked the great drains on purpose. Old Ulf at the pump works cast the iron for him, believing it was tools. Captured wardens are being turned in the Herald's cages; the ones fully turned go grey and join the packs. Maud Sorrel, the oldest warden, went down alone and drowned in the backed-up water.
5. **Now, early winter.** The drains have backed up and the lower levels are flooding. Gorm is starving and stirring: the ground shakes, the wells run sour, the mines are close to collapse. The Herald means to have the god awake by midwinter. The town hires mercenaries to clear the traps and minions so the drains can run. The council borrowed the hire money from Master Pellet; he advanced it and wants it back.
6. **Floor 1.** The Herald attacks at the mine head. Foehammer and the mercenaries kill him. His voice comes up again from below: the foundry will recast him. From then on he taunts the party all the way down, and waits on floor 20, whole and larger, at the last drain.

### What is at stake

- If Gorm wakes, his dreaming stops and the Sleepless breaks through. Gorm does not want this; it is his waking, not his will, that dooms the town.
- If the party fails, the mines collapse and Dunmarrow dies with them.
- If they clear the drains, the water runs, Gorm sleeps on, and the dream keeps the Sleepless out.

### The places

- **The Gullet of Gorm** is the town's old name for the mines. The levels carry miners' names taken from a body (the Skin, the Gut, the Heart, the Dream; the Swallow, the Crop, the Craw, the Windpipe, the Mouth). They are names. The walls are rock, not flesh, and the mines are not the god's body.
- **The Skin (floors 1-5)** is the church's ground: crypts, chapel, chantry. Its dead were raised by the Herald as his first line.
- **The Gut (6-10)** is where the water backs up: flooded, stagnant, full of vermin grown huge in it.
- **The Heart (11-15)** is the old pump works, which once pushed the water down the drains. The Herald turned it into a foundry; its great engine, the Bellows, now casts minions.
- **The Dream (16-20)** is nearest the sleeping god, where the dream is thinnest. The Sleepless's own kind come through here.
- **The Mouth (floor 20)** holds the last great drain, where the water pours down to Gorm.
- **Each act ends at a great drain** guarded by the act's boss: Canon Ludd (5), Mother Nit (10), the Bellows (15), the Herald himself (20). When the boss dies the party turns the sluice wheel and the drain runs. The player must hear that it worked: the shaking eases, the gas clears, the narrator and Maud say so.

## The cast

### Maud Sorrel (`cast_maud`)

**Sex and voice:** old woman. Gacrux, rendered as Wendy. Default delivery: calm.

**Who she is now:** a drain-warden of thirty-eight years who went down alone after the Verger's order, to keep the water moving, and drowned where it had backed up. Gorm keeps her walking. Water runs from her sleeves and hem and never stops. Through her the god's need reaches the party, and her words get clearer each act. She is not a ghost who frightens; she is a kind old workwoman who has not quite noticed she is dead.

**What she wants from the party, by act:**
- **Act 1 (floor 1, her first line):** "Help me clear the grates, dear. The water has to go down." She does not yet know who the water is for.
- **From act 2 (floor 6, where she first speaks clearly):** "Keep the water going down to him. He is thirsty."

**Her need (quest, phase 3):** water for the god. Open a blocked sluice.

**By act:**
- **Act 1, floor 1:** at the mine head, going down on her rounds as if nothing has happened. She does not know she drowned. Her sentences are short and plain but lose their thread; she talks about the work (grates, sluices, "the water has to go down") and does not yet say who the water is for. Unclear means unfinished, never cryptic.
- **Act 2, floor 6:** standing in the backed-up water of the Swallow. She speaks clearly for the first time and says who the water is for: "He is thirsty." She knows she is soaked through and cold, and does not mind. She can point the party to Tamsin, who is salvaging nearby.
- **Act 3, floor 11:** at the pump works. She knows she drowned and says so plainly. She tells them the engine here once sent the water down and now casts things instead, and that the god is weaker every day the third drain stays shut.
- **Act 4, floors 16, 18, 19:** On 16, if Tobit was not told the truth, she carries the Verger's letter (it came down to her on the water) and says the deacon has been taken for the rite. On 18 she sits down and Gorm speaks through her (below). On 19 she steadies the god while the Verger's rite pushes to wake him; the party holds off the waves around her. Her words are fully clear, as herself and as him.

**How she talks:**
- Calls everyone "dear". Warden's working words: rounds, grates, culverts, sluices.
- Unhurried. Never rushes, never raises her voice, faintly amused at her own state ("Wet? I have been wetter.").
- In act 1 she drifts mid-thought and comes back to the work; from act 2 she finishes every sentence.
- Sample (act 1, her first line on floor 1): "Mind the step, dear, it's wet. Help me clear the grates; I'm late on my rounds. The water has to go down. It always has to go down."
- Sample (act 2): "He is thirsty. That's all it is, dear. Thirty-eight years I kept his water moving, and now it stands here going sour."

**When Gorm speaks through her (floor 18, and only where a beat calls for it):** first person, slow and plain. He is starving, half awake and sorry for the damage: the shaking, the sour wells, the people hurt. He never threatens and never commands. He asks for water and for sleep, and says plainly what his waking would let in. Sample: "Forgive the shaking. I am hungry, and hungry things turn over. Bring the water down and I will sleep, and the door stays shut."

**Once the party knows:** she does not react to the clues; she reacts to the water. After each great drain clears she says, plainly, that he drank. After the floor 18 choice she speaks of him as "he" again and knows which way the party chose.

**She would never say:**
1. Anything that wishes the god awake or calls his waking good.
2. A riddle or a prophecy ("you will see", "everyone does, in the end").
3. That she is always dry, asleep or sleepwalking (the old Maud), or a hero's name.

**Keep from the old voice:** "Mind the step", "dear", "thirty-eight years", going down ahead of the party, kindness. **Drop:** dry in the water, "down is where the sleep is", reading to him, the god as a body.

### Alderman Hesketh Crane (`cast_crane`)

**Sex and voice:** man. Umbriel, rendered as Callum (shared with Wren; Crane talks in long clauses). Default delivery: cheerful, the cheer of bluster.

**Who he is now:** Alderman of Dunmarrow and the party's employer. Honest but guilty. This spring, with the Verger's order keeping the wardens out of the mines, he signed the council's order that stopped paying them. The excuse on paper was that wardens who could not go below need not be paid; the truth, which he owns only on floor 17, is that the council was glad of the excuse because it saved money. The council borrowed the hire money from Pellet; he advanced it and wants it back. He did not know what was below. Now the wells are sour and the ground shakes, and he wants it put right before the town asks why the wardens went. He does not betray the party. His arc: bluster, then caught by his own seal, then down in the mines with a shovel.

**What he wants from the party:** "Clear the drains and get the water running. And any council papers you find down there, bring them to me. Unread, if you can manage it."

**His need (quest):** the dismissal orders gathered up before the town reads them (floors 1, 4, 9).

**By act:**
- **Act 1, floors 1 and 4:** On 1, at the mine head just after the Herald falls, he hires the party and explains the job: sour wells, shaking ground, wardens who went down and did not return. He asks for the notices posted at the mine head, for the council's files. On 4 he has come down "to check progress" and finds the wardens' log (clue 1). The Verger's order explains why the wardens stopped working, and he is relieved it was the church and not the council, and says so too quickly.
- **Act 2, floor 9:** at the Cistern. The dismissal order (clue 2) carries his own signature under the council's seal. Caught. The bluster goes; he admits it is his hand. Grisel watches him read it. He gives the official excuse and believes he means it: the Verger had already forbidden anyone to go below, so the council stopped paying wardens who could not work. He does not mention money.
- **Act 3, floors 11-15 (by mention):** back in town, deciding. Preferred carrier: Pellet, who says the alderman has stopped coming to council and has bought a shovel.
- **Act 4, floor 17:** he has come down himself, with a shovel. He owns the truth behind the excuse he gave on 9: the council was glad of the Verger's order because stopping the wardens' pay saved money, and he signed it. He holds a sluice open so the party can get past the gas, and stays holding it. If Grisel was forgiven he can say she and her crew are back on the upper drains.

**How he talks:**
- Acts 1-2: speaks in clauses, accounts and apologies; "council property", "I account for everything", "the council thanks you in writing". Gives orders while apologising for them.
- Self-aware about his cowardice, which is where his humour lives ("I would go myself, but I am, frankly, a coward.").
- After clue 2: short, plain sentences. After act 4 begins: a workman's plainness, out of breath.
- Sample (act 1): "Sour wells, shaking ground, and wardens who went down and never came up. Clear the drains and the council pays in full. Any papers you find are council property."
- Sample (act 2, floor 9): "That's my hand. The Verger had shut the mines. What use is a warden who can't go down? So we stopped paying them. That is what we told ourselves."
- Sample (act 4, floor 17): "We were glad of his order. It saved the wages, and I signed it. Go on through; I'll hold the sluice open as long as my arms do."

**Once the party knows:** after clue 1 he blames the Verger, a little too eagerly. After clue 2 he gives the official excuse but stops blaming anyone else for his signature. After clue 4 (on 17) he is angry at the Verger and at himself in the same breath, and says both.

**He would never say:**
1. Anything that sells out, abandons or lies the party into danger. He does not betray them.
2. After floor 9, that it was all the Verger's fault or the wardens' own.
3. The old plot's words (contract as a trap, names carved, "the usual"), or a hero's name.

**Keep from the old voice:** bluster, "council property", "Don't eat the cloth", the honest coward. **Drop:** the counterfoil, carving names, Michaelmas, the betrayal.

### Deacon Tobit Hale (`cast_tobit`)

**Sex and voice:** young man. Puck, rendered as Hank. Default delivery: warm.

**Who he is now:** a young deacon of Gorm's church in Dunmarrow and the Verger's junior. The Verger went below in spring, Tobit believes to pray for the town, and Tobit has kept the church alone since. He believes that if the town prays properly, Gorm will save it. His arc: prays, doubts, then turns on his master or is taken by him.

**What he wants from the party:** "Pray with me. If we ask him properly, Gorm will save the town." (From act 3, if told the truth: "I need to know if my master did this.")

**His need (quest):** proof of what his master did. Investigate; ends with the letter on floor 16.

**By act:**
- **Act 1, floor 3:** praying in the Nave for the god to save the town, glad of company. The first crushing trap is on this floor. Choice: tell him the god is starving and no help is coming (`told_tobit`), or let him pray. Told: shaken, quiet, then he resolves to find out what happened to the drains. Not told: grateful, and keeps praying.
- **Act 2 (by mention):** he passes Pellet's door going down, asking after his master. Same mention in both branches.
- **Act 3, floor 14:** he has followed the party down to the Quench and found the Verger's vestments thrown off in the water. Told: he asks plainly whether the Verger did this, already fearing yes, and wants proof. Not told (one state, to confirm): he still trusts his master. From the vestments he has concluded that the Verger is the demon's prisoner. A message in the Verger's own hand has summoned him below to help with "a rite that will save the town", and he believes it. He asks the party whether his master is a prisoner. If they press him (the existing Betrayal on this floor), he does not attack them: he runs to the summons, and the Herald's minions sent to escort him are what the party fights. The HUD will say he "has turned", so his last line must make plain that he has gone to them, not become one. If they let him be, he goes on down to the summons. Either way he is "taken for the rite" on 16.
- **Act 4, floors 16 and 19:** Told: on 16 he brings the Verger's letter himself and turns on his master openly; he wants the Verger stopped. Not told: he has been taken for the rite (Maud says so on 16). On 19 he is in the Verger's rite, heard or mentioned, half-changed like the prisoners; breaking the rite lets him go. No new NPC is needed for this.

**How he talks:**
- Polite to a fault, eager, glad to be useful; thorough and says so ("I was thorough. I am always thorough.").
- Church words used sincerely: pray, bless, the Verger, my master.
- Once told the truth: still polite, but he starts asking direct questions and wants direct answers.
- Sample (act 1): "Pray with me, would you? The wells are sour and the ground shakes, but if we ask him properly, Gorm will save the town. He always has."
- Sample (act 3, not told, his last line when pressed): "He's sent for me. A rite to save the town, in his own hand. I'm going to him, and you won't stop me. Those are his escort; don't follow."
- Sample (act 4, told): "I read his letter. 'The way will be open by midwinter.' My master wrote that to the thing in the mines. I want him stopped, and I want to be there."

**Once the party knows:** clue 1 (the Verger's order) reaches him through the party on 14 and is what he is afraid of. Clue 4 breaks him in the told branch and he says it aloud.

**He would never say:**
1. Anything cruel, coarse or mocking. He does not curse.
2. That the god is wicked or meant the town harm, even when he despairs.
3. Old plot words (pilgrims, stations, the congregation being expected), or a hero's name.

**Keep from the old voice:** "I am told I am a good listener", thoroughness as his one virtue, "I would rather be wrong out loud than obedient in a whisper." **Drop:** the singing part held very long, the Choir, the psalter's lever parity as a plot device.

### Grisel Thorn (`cast_grisel`)

**Sex and voice:** woman. Despina, rendered as Abby. Default delivery: weary.

**Who she is now:** the last drain-warden still free. When the wells began to sour this spring, she took her crew down unpaid, against the Verger's order, to clear the drains. The Herald's minions took them. She ran and left them. She has hidden since in a camp in the Crop, near the drains, keeping one seized pump turning when she can. She wants her crew back, or to be forgiven for leaving them.

**What she wants from the party (floor 7, her first line):** "Keep those things off me while I get this pump turning. Then help me find my crew; that orc has them caged somewhere below." She does not say she left them; that confession is held for floor 9.

**Her need (quest):** her crew back before they turn. Free them on floor 12.

**By act:**
- **Act 1 (by mention):** the wardens freed on floor 2 say Grisel Thorn's crew went down first and nobody has seen them since.
- **Act 2, floors 7 and 9:** On 7, minions are holding a rite to hurry the god awake; Grisel works a seized pump near her camp to push water down while the party holds them off. Getting the pump turning breaks the rite. She asks for help finding her crew and says no more about them. On 9 she watches Crane read his own signature, then admits she left her crew behind. - **Act 3, floor 12:** her crew are caged in the Casting Hall, further gone than the prisoners on floor 2 because they were taken first: nearly grey, nearly pack. Not forgiven: she has taken the Herald's keys to save herself and stands as his head jailer; the party takes the keys from her and she does not survive the floor.
- **Act 4 (by mention):** Forgiven: she and what is left of her crew are back at the upper drains (Crane can say so on 17; the ending narration can too). Not forgiven: the Herald mocks the warden who sold herself for a ring of keys.

**How she talks:**

- Never asks for pity; dark humour aimed at herself.

- Sample (act 3, forgiven): "That's my crew. Grey, but that's them. Keep the jailers off me and I'll bring them out one at a time."

**Once the party knows:** after clue 2 she is bitter at the council for dropping its wardens (her crew went down unpaid), but never more bitter than she is at herself. Before floor 17 she does not say the council did it for money.

**She would never say:**
1. Self-pity, or a request to be pitied.
2. A word against her crew.
3. That she sold or handed over her crew (the old plot); she ran. And never a hero's name.

**Keep from the old voice:** flat and wry, "Silence is also an answer. A coward's, but an answer.", keeping a camp alone. **Drop:** nineteen years alone, walking her four to the jailers, the spider's-shell hut as a home of decades.

### Old Ulf (`cast_ulf`)

**Sex and voice:** old man. Orus, rendered as Hamish (shared with Brand; Ulf is chatty and warm). Default delivery: cheerful.

**Who he is now:** keeper of the old pump works, eighty, whose engine once pushed the water down the drains. He turned the engine to casting because the pay was good and nobody else wanted his work. His eyes are going, and he never saw the whole of what he cast. He wants good work and a paying customer, until floor 13 shows him what he made.

**What he wants from the party:** "Off the sand! I've an order to pour by morning for a customer who pays in gold. Fetch me coal or get out of my light."

**His need (quest):** to undo what he made. Break the moulds on floor 13.

**By act:**
- **Act 1 (by mention):** someone (the freed wardens on floor 2, or Crane) says Old Ulf still keeps the pump works and nobody has heard from him since spring.
- **Act 2 (by mention):** Pellet says the pump works has been buying coal on Ulf's account all season, and Ulf never paid for coal in his life.
- **Act 3, floors 11 and 13:** On 11, cheerful and proud, casting "tools" for a customer who pays well; his journeyman Oskar knows better and joins the party. On 13 (clue 3) the moulds are the crushing walls, the spike beds, the iron minions, and the Herald's own mould, larger. He finally sees what he has been making. Horrified, he wants them broken.
- **Act 4 (by mention):** he stays at the pump works breaking his moulds one by one; Oskar or Pellet says so. (In phase 3 he may forge a weapon from the party's boons.)

**How he talks:**
- Scolds and fusses about his workshop ("Off the sand!", "Mind the shelves"). Calls the party "you lot".
- Craft pride: every casting is "good work", and he will tell you why.
- After floor 13: short and broken, no jokes, no pride.
- Sample (act 3, before the moulds): see the want sentence above.
- Sample (after the moulds): "That's a crushing wall. That's a spike bed. That one's him, the orc, only bigger. I poured every one and called it good work. Help me break them."

**Once the party knows:** clue 3 is his turn. Before it he never suspects; after it he never makes an excuse.

**He would never say:**

3. The old plot's faces in the sand (unborn townsfolk, children coming out slow), or a hero's name.

### Master Oswin Pellet (`cast_pellet`, the merchant)

**Who he is now:** a Dunmarrow factor. The council borrowed the hire money from him; he advanced it and wants it back. He pays it out to the party himself, less his cut: a hundred and fifty gold. The mine carts were his trade, and the trade has stopped. He follows the party down and trades from behind a locked door on every floor. He wants his money back and the trade reopened. His arc is already in his 68 recorded lines: bluster, unease, fear. Most of them stay.

**What he wants from the party:** "Clear the mines and I get my money back and my carts running again. Until then, sell to me, buy from me, and keep every receipt."

**His need (quest):** his stock back from the bandits on floor 8.

**By act:**
- **Act 1, floors 1-5, bluster:** pays out the advance, talks up investments and particulars, keeps receipts, is proud of his lock. Speculating on fine dry lodgings in the cleared tunnels is bluster that fits.
- **Act 2, floors 6-10, unease:** the quakes keep time; carriers ask questions; on 8 the bandits the Herald pays have looted his stock and he wants it back. On 9 he is glad to see the party in a way he does not like admitting. Carrier of the act 2 mentions: Tobit passing his door, Ulf's coal account.
- **Act 3, floors 11-15, fear:** a forge he never bought, doors he never paid for, footsteps after his own. Carrier of the act 3 mention that the alderman has bought a shovel.
- **Act 4, floors 16-20, fear and something better:** selling the good stock because there will be no better day; on 20 he asks the party to come back up afterwards, the first thing he asks for that is not money.

**How he talks:**
- Everything is a price, a receipt or a backer. Courage in sentences, none anywhere else.
- Fear shows as counting: coins, steps, footsteps, exits.
- Short shop patter for greetings and trade; one small joke at his own expense.
- Sample (existing, keep): "Name your price and I'll name mine, and then we'll use mine."
- Sample (act 2, floor 8): "Bandits in my stock, paid by that orc to keep the town out. Bring it back and I'll knock something off the price. Not much. Something."

**Once the party knows:** after clue 2 he is quietly furious that the council dropped its wardens and then came to him to borrow the money to put it right. Only from floor 17 does he say the council did it to save money ("saving on wages and borrowing from me"). He never learns or says who let the demon in.

**He would never say:**
1. Who let the demon in (the Verger). He never states the answer to the mystery.
2. Anything brave in deed: he never offers to fight or to come out from behind the door.
3. "Inspectors", "gold teeth in the walls", or anything else from the old plot; or a hero's name.

**Keep:** the locked door, receipts, backers, "I am paying for the door", the lodgings bluster, the whole fear arc. **Rewrite:** only lines that name the old plot (for example "Ah, my inspectors!" on floor 2 and the gold teeth on floor 6).

### Absalom Quill, the Verger (`cast_quill`)

**Sex and voice:** man. Sadaltager, rendered as Conrad (shared with Oskar; the Verger talks in long, precise sentences). Default delivery: calm.

**Who he is now:** the Verger of Gorm's church in Dunmarrow for forty years, of great age, keeper of the church and of the crypt door that leads to the mines. He prayed to Gorm all his life and was never answered. The Herald answered him and promised that the god who comes through when Gorm wakes will reward the man who opened the door. He let the demon in, ordered "no one below", and wrote to the Herald: "The way will be open by midwinter." He is no longer the voice that taunts the party; the Herald is. The party hears about him all the way down and meets him on floor 19.

**What he wants from the party:** "Stand aside and let him wake. I have earned a god who answers."

**By act:**
- **Act 1:** not seen. Tobit, on 3, says his master went below to pray for the town. On 4 the wardens' log ends "By the Verger's order, no one below."
- **Act 2 (by mention):** on 9 Crane says the council saw no point paying wardens the Verger had shut out of the mines.
- **Act 3, floor 14:** his vestments lie in the water at the Quench, thrown off. He has given up the church.
- **Act 4, floors 16, 17, 19, 20:** On 16 his letter (clue 4) is read. On 17 Crane names him. On 19 he conducts the rite to wake the god while Maud steadies the god against it; he speaks to the party but is not fought; the church's relic is in his hands (see Sister Ione); Tobit is in the rite if he was not told the truth. When the rite breaks he is left kneeling with nothing answering him. On 20 the Herald speaks of him with contempt.

**How he talks:**
- Dry, precise, liturgical cadence; long patient sentences that still fit in 220 characters.
- Offended by noise, mess and blood; never raises his voice. His humour is fastidiousness.
- Speaks of Gorm as a master who never paid his servant.
- Sample: "Do keep your voices down. Forty years I swept his church and he never once answered me. Something else has. Stand aside and let him wake."

**Once the party knows:** on 19 the party holds the letter. He knows it was read and is not ashamed of it; he is annoyed it was read aloud.

**He would never say:**
1. A curse or a raised word. The old coarse taunts were his; they are the Herald's now.
2. Remorse, or "I was deceived". He believes to the end.
3. A floor taunt, a word that calls the Herald his master (he thinks of him as his guest), or a hero's name.

**Keep from the old voice:** precise, offended by blood on the flagstones, "Wipe your feet. This is a church." **Drop:** the stations, the congregation, keeping the god asleep, four every generation.

### Morg Ashtusk, the Herald (the orc, the antagonist)

**Sex and voice:** man. Fenrir, rendered as Dominus (the stock villain voice; nobody else uses it). Default delivery: mocking; booming when he announces; never pleading.

**Who he is now:** a huge orc chieftain, the chief minion and herald of the Sleepless. He came in through the church's crypts, which the Verger opened for him, and took the mines as his master's base. He raised the church's dead, laid the traps, choked the great drains, filled the tunnels with minions to keep the townspeople off the drains, and is turning the captured wardens into more. He wants the mines, and the god awake by midwinter, so the Sleepless can come through. He speaks every taunt the party hears on every floor, the sealed-stairs line and the portal lines, and the floor 20 boss lines.

**Required facts:**
- **He was killed on floor 1 and recast.** Foehammer and the mercenaries kill him at the mine head. His voice comes straight back up from below: the foundry will recast him. His mould is among the moulds on floor 13. He waits on floor 20 whole, larger, in the same body.
- **He serves the Sleepless.** He speaks of his master with complete devotion and never doubts it will come through.
- **He mocks the party as hired help:** hirelings, day labour, the town's hired shovels, paid by the floor. He prices their courage in wages.
- **He never begs.** Not at half health, not dying, not in any branch.

**What he wants from the party:** "Go home, hirelings. These mines are my master's now, and your god is going to wake. You are not paid enough to stop either." (He does not name midwinter before the letter is read on floor 16.)

**By act:**
- **Act 1, floors 1-5:** attacks at the mine head and is killed; from then on a voice from below. Boasts of his traps and his raised dead; calls the jailbreak prisoners his workforce; Canon Ludd is his first lieutenant. When the first drain clears he sneers and promises worse.
- **Act 2, floors 6-10:** enjoys the water backing up and the god starving; jokes about the town's sour wells. His minions hold the rite on 7. He pays the bandits on 8. After clue 2 he mocks the town for sacking its own wardens (not yet for money; that waits for floor 17). Mother Nit breeds in his stagnant water.
- **Act 3, floors 11-15:** his voice comes from the foundry where he is being recast. Proud of the castings and of "my founder", the old man who cast them without looking. On 12 he gloats over the turned crew (and, if Grisel was not forgiven, over the warden who took his keys). After clue 3 he stops pretending: yes, the iron is his, and so is the mould for a bigger him.
- **Act 4, floors 16-20:** whole again, waiting at the Mouth. After clue 4 he laughs at the Verger ("priests always write it down"). He sneers at Crane holding the sluice on 17. After the floor 18 choice: with `wake_gorm` he gloats that the dream is thin and his master is close; with `let_him_dream` he is contemptuous of a god too frightened to wake. On 19 he scorns the Verger's failed rite. On 20 he fights at the last drain. Half health and death lines change on `wake_gorm`: Gorm, half awake, strikes at him, and he dies defiant, saying his master is almost through. Without it he dies contemptuous, saying the Sleepless will send another.

**How he talks:**
- Short, plain, declarative sentences in full grammar. No broken "orc-speak", no grunts written out.
- Contempt measured in money: wages, fees, what the town is paying for each of them.
- Proud of his craft: traps, iron, the drains he choked. He explains nothing he has not already done.
- Coarse words (bloody, sod, bugger) only in the cursing version of a taunt; every taunt has a clean twin with the same meaning and no coarse word.
- Sample (taunt): "Still walking, hirelings? The town paid you for a day's work. I built these halls to outlast your wages."
- Sample (floor 20 intro): "Killed me at the gate, didn't you? I came back bigger. Turn that sluice wheel if you can reach it, hirelings. My master is listening."

**Once the party knows:** he never reveals a clue before the party finds it. After each clue he rubs it in: the Verger's order (4), the council's sacking (9), the moulds and his own (13), the letter (16), and from 17 that the council did it to save money. He also knows the turned join his packs, and says so from floor 2. His taunts also follow `told_tobit`, `grisel_forgiven` and `wake_gorm`.

**He would never say:**
1. A plea, a surrender or a bargain for his life.
2. A riddle, or a word of respect for Gorm or the party. He calls Gorm "your god" or "the sleeper".
3. Anything that makes the Verger his master or his equal, or a hero's name (the `{name}` and `{party}` placeholders are fine).

**Voice engineering note:** floor 20's boss lines are spoken through the stock boss voice slot today (`AdventureDirector.cs:112` enqueues them with `Voices.Boss`). For the Herald to sound the same on floor 20 as in his taunts, the bake or the engine must give those lines Fenrir.

## The heroes

The four heroes are mercenaries, hired for pay. They already have their voices: about a thousand barks, 212 banter lines and twenty victory-dance lines each, and the owner likes them ("many of those lines are good and don't need to be changed"). These sheets describe those voices as they are and add one thread: over the four acts each hero moves from "it's a job" to caring whether the town lives. The caring is never a speech. It shows as a joke that lands differently, a job done that nobody is paying for, a captive not left behind.

Rules for hero lines:
- Keep every existing line unless it refers to the removed plot (plumbing, inspectors, nineteen years, names carved, the contract as a trap, the Verger as the one in charge) or a new line clearly helps the story.
- No dance line is removed. Dances are fun, vain and quick; a replacement must be funnier.
- New lines move the thread one small step per act, in the hero's existing manner.
- The heroes may name each other in banter, as now. No one else names them.

### Brand (warrior)

**Sex and voice:** man. Orus, rendered as Hamish (shared with Old Ulf). Default delivery: weary.

**Who he is:** an old soldier, thirty years in, "Corporal. Twice. They kept taking it back." He walks in front with his shield and counts doors. Under the grumbling he has always brought his people home ("Eleven. Alive. I made sure of that.").

**What he wants from the party:** "Pay us, point us at it, and stay behind me."

**The thread, by act:**
- **Act 1:** a job; complains about the walking, the rations and the alderman. On floor 2 the freed wardens tell him the finished ones go grey and join the packs, so some of what he kills used to be townspeople. It bothers him more than he says.
- **Act 2:** after clue 2, a soldier's contempt for a council that abandoned its own wardens (from floor 17 he can add that it was for a wage).
- **Act 3:** on floor 12 he sees Grisel's crew nearly gone and cannot pretend any more. Quieter. Nobody gets left in a cage.
- **Act 4:** finishing it is no longer about the pay, and he would never say so in those words.

**How he talks:** clipped imperatives; soldier's terms (flank, line, shield up); deadpan; counts things. Sample (new thread, floor 12): "Cages first, loot after. Those were drain-diggers this spring. Nobody gets left."

**He would never say:** 1. A speech of more than two sentences about why it matters. 2. Anything that leaves a captive or a comrade behind. 3. Long or fancy words (that is Ilex).

**Keep:** "Shield up, feet planted, breathe out.", the doors, "It's always Tuesday down here", every dance. **Look at:** "Nobody said god. They said plumbing.", "Nineteen years of fools taught me that trick.", "At this rate the Verger dies of old age before we find him."

**Keep:** "Booty be all that", "Knock first", the pockets, every dance. **Look at:** "Gold teeth. The god won't miss a molar.", "Don't let the verger write my name neat."

### Wren (ranger)

**Sex and voice:** man. Umbriel, rendered as Callum (shared with Crane). Default delivery: weary or calm.

### Ilex (mage)

**Sex and voice:** man. Iapetus, rendered as Theodore. Default delivery: calm.

**Who he is:** an academy mage, pompous and curious; "Observe.", "Fascinating."; writing a paper on everything; names the puddles (Gerald, Gerald's widow).

**What he wants from the party:** "Observe, record, and, if strictly necessary, set it on fire."

**The thread, by act:**
- **Act 1:** the god is a subject for a monograph.
- **Act 2:** the water rises and Gerald grows; the puddle jokes fit this story better than the last one. A dead drain is a fine joke until it is a town.
