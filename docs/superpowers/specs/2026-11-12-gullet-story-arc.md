# The Gullet of Gorm: story rewire (design)

Status: storyline approved by the owner on 2026-11-12 ("this new story works better", "let's rewire the story with the new storyline"). Nothing in the game has been changed yet.
The premise is the owner's. Lines marked (suggested) and the list under "Decisions made for the owner" are fill-ins.

## Why

The shipped campaign was planned floor by floor by an AI. The owner's verdict: there is no real story to follow, the characters' wording makes no sense, and their individual stories do not connect to the whole. This design replaces the plot with one chain of cause and effect, gives every character a plain need tied to it, and makes clearing the drains the visible point of the game.

## The story in brief

A good god, Gorm, sleeps under the town of Dunmarrow, and the water that runs down through the old mines feeds him.
His dreams are what keep evil away from this world.
For three hundred years the townspeople have kept the mine drains clear so that water keeps flowing.
Now a demon, an orc chieftain serving an evil god, has seized the mines as a base for its master's takeover of this world.
It has filled the tunnels with traps and lesser minions so nobody can reach the drains.
The drains have backed up, the lower levels are flooding, and Gorm, starved of water, is stirring: the mines shake and the wells above run sour.
The town hires a team of mercenaries to clear out the traps and minions so the drains can run again.

## What is at stake

- If the god wakes, his dreaming stops. The dreams are the barrier that holds the evil god back, so the evil god breaks through and the town is destroyed. Gorm means the town no harm; it is his waking, not his will, that dooms it.
- If the heroes fail, the mines collapse and the town perishes.
- If they succeed, the water runs, the god sleeps on, and his dreams go on keeping evil away.

## The townspeople

The people the party meets below are from Dunmarrow. They are either trying to help or they are trapped.

- **Helping:** the drain-wardens who went down to do their job, Crane, Tobit, Oskar, Sister Ione, Foehammer.
- **Trapped:** the prisoners. The orc is turning them into minions. That is why they look so bad and why they have come to look alike: the change wears away who they were. Freeing them in time saves them; the ones nobody reaches become the minions of the next floors.

This makes the jailbreak floors rescues with a clock, and it tells the player that some of what they are killing used to be townspeople.

## Cause and effect

| What the player sees | Why |
|---|---|
| The orc attacks on floor 1 | It is the chief minion, organising the assault |
| Traps and minions on every floor | Put there by the orc to keep the townspeople from the drains |
| Prisoners who look wretched and alike | They are townspeople being turned into minions |
| Flooded lower levels | The drains are blocked and the water is backing up |
| Foul gas on some floors | Stagnant water and rot where the drains no longer run (suggested) |
| Tremors and falling rock | The god is starving, and stirs |
| The town cannot do the job itself | The mines are now too dangerous for ordinary workers |
| The orc wants the god awake | Gorm's dreams are what keep evil away. If he wakes, the dream barrier falls and the evil god breaks through. |

## The final choice (suggested)

The game already has a choice on floor 18 that changes floors 19 and 20. Because the dream barrier falls if he wakes, the party can never choose to wake him. To keep a choice there, it becomes a question of how much to ask of him:

- **"Sleep. We will finish it."** The god stays deep under. The last fight is harder, and the town is saved whole.
- **"Lend us what strength you have."** Gorm, half awake, strikes at the demon in his sleep. The last fight is easier, but he is closer to waking and the dream barrier thins: the mines shake worse on floors 19 and 20 and more of the demon's kind get through.

If that is not wanted, floor 18 becomes a scene with no choice and the ending is a straight fight.

**Companions:**
- Foehammer fought the orc first and wants to finish it.
- Tamsin Rook came for salvage and stays for the pay.
- Oskar Vell cast things for the orc and wants to break them.
- Sister Ione is ashamed of what the Verger has done to her church.

**The four heroes** are mercenaries hired for pay. Their banter should move from "it's a job" to caring whether the town lives. Nothing in the plot depends on their names, because co-op players name their own.

## Writing rules

1. Every character states what they want the first time they speak.
2. Every character appears at least once in every act.
3. No riddles. One image per speech at most.
4. Characters speak differently once the party holds a clue.
5. The Verger's taunts follow what the party has learned.

## What this changes

Unchanged: the twenty floors, their titles, themes, objectives, puzzles, the tier bosses, the cast and their voices, and the three choice points.

Changed:
- The secret that the town sacrifices four people every nineteen years is removed.
- The orc replaces the Verger as the last boss. The Verger moves to the rite on floor 19. This needs engine work, not only words.
- The four clues keep their floors but become the log, the dismissal order, the moulds and the letter.
- Crane no longer betrays the party.
- Nearly every spoken line is rewritten and re-recorded.

## Every NPC has a need, a quest and a boon

Owner's direction: every NPC's story follows the same shape and supports the main arc, and each has a need or mini quest that leads to a boon; several boons lead to a powerful new weapon.

1. The demon's takeover has cost them something.
2. They ask the party for one thing, in plain words, the first time they speak.
3. Doing it is a short quest on that floor or a later one, built from the existing quest templates (recover, escort, hold out, investigate, kill a target).
4. They give a boon in thanks, and their part of the story moves on.

| NPC | Need | Quest |
|---|---|---|
| Alderman Crane | The dismissal orders gathered up before the town reads them | Recover the papers (floors 1, 4, 9) |
| Master Pellet | His stock back from the bandits | Recover it on floor 8 |
| Deacon Tobit | Proof of what his master did | Investigate; ends with the letter on floor 16 |
| Grisel Thorn | Her crew back before they turn | Free them on floor 12 |
| Old Ulf | To undo what he made | Break the moulds on floor 13 |
| Maud Sorrel | Water for the god | Open a blocked sluice |
| Foehammer | The orc dead | Carried to floor 20 |
| Tamsin Rook | A missing partner's body and tools | Recover |
| Oskar Vell | The casting he was forced to make destroyed | Kill a target |
| Sister Ione | The church's relic out of the Verger's hands | Recover |

**Boons (builder's call, marked as such):** an NPC's boon is a lasting blessing on the party of the same kind the shrines already give, named for the giver ("the Warden's Thanks"). It reuses the existing boon system; it is not a new item type.

**Weapons (builder's call):** when the party holds three NPC boons, Old Ulf or Oskar forges one named legendary weapon from them at the pump works (or Pellet delivers it if the party is already past floor 15). Four weapons in all, one suited to each class. This is a quest reward handed over by a character, not a crafting system: there are no recipes, materials or crafting screen.

## How the heroes clear the drains

Built from parts the game already has.

| Way | What the player does | Built on |
|---|---|---|
| Open the sluice | Find and pull the levers to raise the gate | The shifting-vault lever puzzle |
| Break the blockage | Smash the debris and nests packed into the channel | Breakable props and nests |
| Hold the pump | Start it, then defend it against waves until the water runs | The ritual objective |
| Beat the foul air | Reach and open the drain before the gas fills the floor | The rising-gas objective |
| Guard a warden | Free a drain-warden and protect them while they work | Jailbreak and the escort quest |
| Fetch the crank | Recover the sluice wheel's handle | The recover quest |

- **Ordinary floors:** the four existing objectives are reworded as drain work. Same play.
- **Boss floors (5, 10, 15, 20):** the boss guards one of the four great drains. When it dies the party turns the sluice wheel. That ends the act.
- **The player must see it work:** the shaking eases, gas clears, the narrator and Maud say so. Water visibly dropping on flooded floors is wanted but is the one piece of new engine work; it goes in a later phase.

## Lines, dances and voice

Owner's direction:
- **Keep the heroes' victory dances** when they clear enemies, and make them fun. The dance lines stay or get funnier; none are removed.
- **Many hero lines are good and stay.** A hero line is rewritten only if it refers to the removed plot (the nineteen-year sacrifice, "plumbers", "inspectors", names carved on a stone, the contract as a trap) or if a new line helps the story.
- **Use the NPCs' existing voices and re-record all new audio.** Every rewritten or new line is voiced with the speaker's current voice. Lines that do not change keep their recordings.
- The orc is a new speaker and needs a voice (builder's call: the stock villain voice, since the Verger keeps his own).

Scale today: about 250 lines spoken by floor NPCs and narration, 54 villain taunts, 68 merchant lines, 212 banter lines and about 1,000 hero barks. The first four groups are largely rewritten; the barks are mostly kept. The last full voice bake (1,162 lines) cost under two dollars, so the re-record sits well inside the fifteen-dollar cap.

## Phases

1. **Words and voice.** Rewrite the campaign file and the twenty floor files by hand inside the existing format: synopsis, cast, clues, tier lines, narration, NPC dialogue, villain taunts, merchant lines, the affected banter and barks, dance lines. Make the orc the floor 20 boss and the Verger the celebrant of the floor 19 rite. Re-record. No new systems.
2. **Drains made real.** Objective wording, the sluice wheel after each boss, the feedback when a drain clears, prisoners shown as turning.
3. **NPC quests, boons and weapons.**
4. **Falling water** on flooded floors.

Each phase ships on its own and leaves the campaign playable.

## Constraints

- The campaign is rewritten by hand in the shipped files. It is not re-baked by the AI planner, which would regenerate every floor.
- Floor layouts, seeds and layout hashes do not change in phase 1; the existing checks that every floor loads and every voiced line resolves must still pass.
- The three choice flags keep their ids (`told_tobit`, `grisel_forgiven`, `wake_gorm`) so saves and wiring are untouched; only their meaning in the text changes.
- Every line is written in the speaker's own sex and voiced with the voice already assigned to them.
- No new 3D art, no installers, no crafting system.

## Settled after review (builder's calls unless marked)

- **Voice is part of phase 1 (owner's direction).** Phase 1 ends with every rewritten line recorded. The earlier sentence that new lines stay unvoiced is withdrawn. The older "no voice spend" rule applied to the Depths update, not to this work.
- **The ritual floors keep their play in phase 1.** The party still defends a friendly figure against waves. Floor 7: Grisel works a seized pump while the party holds off the minions. Floor 19: Maud steadies the god while the Verger's rite to wake him pushes the other way; he is heard, not fought. "Breaking a rite" as a new kind of objective is not built.
- **The floor 1 orc can be killed, and that is the story.** The party kills Morg at the mine head. He is the evil god's chief minion and is recast in the foundry: the moulds on floor 13 include his. He waits on floor 20 in his true form, the owner's model `big_baddy.glb` (owner's decision 2026-11-12); floor 1 keeps `orc1.glb`. No scripted retreat is needed. A retreat can be added later if wanted.
- **The floor 18 choice is words only in phase 1.** It changes the lines on floors 19 and 20. Making the last fight easier or harder is phase 2 work. "Lend us your strength" sets `wake_gorm`; "sleep" sets `let_him_dream`.
- **The prisoners' clock is framing, not a timer.** The jailbreak floors cannot be failed today and stay that way. Turning is shown in what the prisoners and jailers say, and from phase 2 by a tint on the prisoner bodies.
- **Writing rule 2 is relaxed:** a character must be present in every act by a scene, a letter, a mention or a voice. Only the cast and the Verger are covered, not the companions.
- **Weapons:** one forged weapon for every three NPC boons, so up to three in a run; it goes to the party's shared loot. Details wait for phase 3.
- **Stale recordings** of the old AI planning calls under `data/adventures/builtin/authored` are left alone.

## The taunting voice (owner's decision, 2026-11-12)

The voice that taunts the party all the way down is **the orc's**. Morg becomes the campaign's antagonist: every taunt, the sealed-stairs line and the portal lines are his, rewritten and re-recorded. The Verger becomes a cast character the party meets and catches out; he keeps his own voice for his own lines.

**Voices do not change (owner's rule):** every NPC and every hero keeps the text-to-speech voice already assigned to them. The assignments are recorded in the project's voice table. The orc is the only new speaker; he takes the stock villain voice (Gemini name Fenrir, rendered as Inworld Dominus), which no other character uses.

## Decisions made for the owner (check these)

1. The orc is **Morg Ashtusk, the Herald**; its god is **the Sleepless**.
2. The floor 18 choice is kept as "sleep" or "lend us your strength".
3. The town carries one fault: the council let the drain-wardens go to save money.
4. NPC boons are blessings of the existing kind; three of them earn a forged weapon.
5. Drains are cleared both ways: by fighting on boss floors, by working things out on ordinary floors.
